# Unit Converter — Examples

## Length: miles to kilometers and back

This is the one you'll use the most if you travel between the US and anywhere else.

| Input | From | To | Result | When you'd use this |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | km | miles | 62.14 | Reading road signs in Europe |
| 26.2 | miles | km | 42.16 | Converting a marathon distance |
| 6 | feet | m | 1.829 | Figuring out ceiling height in a European apartment listing |
| 180 | cm | feet+inches | 5' 11" | Converting your height for a US form |

The marathon one is fun: 26.2 miles is exactly 42.195 km, which is the standard marathon distance. The 42.2 km number didn't come from a metric origin — it came from the 1908 London Olympics route.

## Mass: body weight and cooking

| Input | From | To | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 70 | kg | lb | 154.3 |
| 150 | lb | kg | 68.04 |
| 500 | g | oz | 17.64 |
| 1 | oz | g | 28.35 |

A 70 kg person weighs 154.3 lb. A 150 lb person is 68.04 kg. For cooking: 500 g of flour is about 17.6 oz, or roughly 4.4 US cups — useful when converting European recipes.

## Temperature: the benchmarks

Some temperature points come up often enough to memorize, but the converter is there for everything in between.

| Input | From | To | Result | Why you'd care |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | degC | degF | 212 | Boiling water |
| 0 | degC | degF | 32 | Freezing water |
| 37 | degC | degF | 98.6 | Body temperature |
| -40 | degC | degF | -40 | The one point where Celsius and Fahrenheit agree |
| 20 | degC | degF | 68 | Pleasant room temperature |
| 180 | degC | degF | 356 | Typical oven temp for baking |

The -40 crossover is a good bar trivia fact. At -40 degrees, both scales read the same number. The formula breaks even because (-40 x 9/5) + 32 = -40.

## Speed: driving in different countries

| Input | From | To | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | mph | km/h | 96.56 |
| 100 | km/h | mph | 62.14 |
| 130 | km/h | mph | 80.78 |
| 1 | knot | km/h | 1.852 |

European highway limits are typically 130 km/h, which is about 81 mph — faster than most US speed limits. A knot is one nautical mile per hour, used in aviation and maritime navigation. The nautical mile is based on the Earth's circumference (one minute of latitude), not an arbitrary rod length — which is why it survives in navigation.

## Data storage: marketing vs reality

| Input | From | To | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | TB | GiB | 931.3 |
| 512 | GB | GiB | 476.8 |
| 256 | GB | GiB | 238.4 |
| 16 | GB | GiB | 14.90 |

If you bought a 512 GB SSD and your OS says it has 476 GB available, you haven't been ripped off. The manufacturer sold you 512 x 10^9 bytes = 512,000,000,000 bytes. The OS reports that as 476.8 GiB. Same number of bytes, different units.

## Volume: cooking conversions

| Input | From | To | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | US cup | mL | 236.6 |
| 1 | US tbsp | mL | 14.79 |
| 1 | US gallon | L | 3.785 |
| 500 | mL | US cups | 2.113 |

American recipes use volume measurements almost exclusively (cups, tablespoons, teaspoons), while pretty much everywhere else uses weight (grams) or metric volume (mL). A "cup of flour" can vary wildly in actual mass depending on how packed it is — which is why serious bakers use weight. The converter at least gets the volume units right.

Note that US and UK fluid ounces are different (29.57 mL vs 28.41 mL), and US and UK gallons are different too (3.785 L vs 4.546 L). The converter uses US units by default — if you're converting a UK recipe, make sure you've got the right gallon.
